Mens Augustini De Statu Naturae Inocentis

By Fulgenzio Bellelli

In the beginning, when God created humanity in His own image, the state of innocent nature was characterized by an extraordinary harmony. The body was perfectly subject to the soul, and the soul to God. This original righteousness was not merely a natural endowment, but a gratuitous gift of divine grace.

According to the mind of Saint Augustine, the grace bestowed upon Adam in the Garden of Eden was sufficient to maintain his innocence, yet allowed for the possibility of falling. It was a grace that enabled him not to sin (posse non peccare), distinct from the glorious grace of the blessed in heaven, which makes it impossible to sin (non posse peccare).

The subjection of the lower appetites to reason in this state was absolute. There was no concupiscence rebelling against the mind, no sorrow, and no fear of death. The state of nature in its innocence was thus a testament to the supreme goodness of the Creator, reflecting a perfect order that was tragically disrupted by the original transgression.

Therefore, we must clearly distinguish between what belongs to the nature of man as a creature, and what was added to him as a special prerogative of grace in the state of innocence. The loss of this original justice through sin left human nature wounded, in profound need of the redemptive grace of Christ to restore the lost harmony and lead humanity to its ultimate supernatural end.
